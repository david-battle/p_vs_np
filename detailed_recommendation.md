The CLO24 template is: a "favorite lower bound" — typically one whose proof is essentially a counting / injection argument — attaches to a pigeonhole-principle variant over PV₁ (they used WPHP, dWPHP, WPHP_WIT). So the productive filter is: pick a lower bound whose proof already smells like "you can't fit that many things in here." That's the signature of the WPHP family, and it makes the equivalence plausible in both directions.
Here are concrete candidates, roughly in my order of preference.
Best bets (clean counting flavor, likely on-theme)
1. Shannon's existence lower bound — "there is a Boolean function of circuit complexity ≥ 2ⁿ/n" (existence, not explicit). The proof is pure pigeonhole: more functions than circuits ⇒ some function is hard. Over PV₁ this is almost literally a surjective/weak pigeonhole statement ("can't cover all truth tables with few circuits"). Natural candidate to be equivalent to surj-WPHP(PV) or WPHP(PV). Low technique risk because the counting is already the principle.
2. Time-bounded Kolmogorov / incompressibility — "∃x, K^t(x) ≥ |x|" (or MCSP hardness). The incompressibility argument is the pigeonhole principle in its purest form ("more strings than short descriptions"). This is the strongest tie to your notes: it connects §6's meta-complexity anchor (Liu–Pass, K^t) directly to the CLO24 pigeonhole thread. If you can pin "there exists an incompressible string of length n" as equivalent to some WPHP variant over PV₁, you've built the missing bridge between two threads you already care about. Genuinely novel angle; specialists in bounded arithmetic rarely think in terms of K^t.
3. Parity formula lower bound (Ω(n²) leaves, Khrapchenko / Andreev). The counting is over subfunctions / leaf sets. Simpler and more canonical than Andreev's near-cubic bound CLO24 already speculated about — a good "first independent calibration" target before attacking their harder Andreev guess.
Good, but more risk
4. Nondeterministic time hierarchy (NTIMEn^c ⊈ NTIMEn^d). Already listed as open in CLO24, so it's a known-good target; but the lazy-diagonalization structure is less obviously pigeonhole-flavored, so the "equivalence class" is genuinely unclear. Higher ceiling, higher uncertainty.
5. Communication complexity of Inner Product mod 2. Set Disjointness ↔ WPHP_WIT is done; IP is the other canonical hard communication problem and its proof is spectral/rank-counting. Might land on the same principle (less interesting) or a genuinely different one (very interesting). Cheap to check.
6. Time–space tradeoffs (e.g., the Ω(n²/log n) tradeoff for SAT, or for palindromes). Pebbling/counting arguments — plausibly WPHP-adjacent, but formalization is heavier.
What I'd avoid first
- Razborov's monotone CLIQUE lower bound — the approximation/sunflower method is much more than counting; unlikely to be a clean pigeonhole equivalence. High effort, low fit.
- Razborov–Smolensky AC⁰2 parity lower bound — polynomial method over finite fields; again more than counting.
My recommendation: start with #2 (K^t incompressibility) or #1 (Shannon existence). Both are canonical, minimal to formalize, sit squarely on the WPHP family, and #2 in particular fuses two threads already central to your notes. The natural first step is to check whether "∃x, K^t(x) ≥ n" is equivalent to or merely implies the relevant WPHP over PV₁ — the two-way direction is where a publishable result hides.
